Hyperlanes

Join two worlds of the running game with a new hyperlane. Click one world, then another (the map is the same way up as in the game). A lane can't cross another lane or pass right by a third world. Both worlds earn the extra income a hyperlane brings (home worlds stay the same), and players see "New hyperlane" in their log.

Add a late player

A friend can join the game while it's running. They get a new home world with no hyperlanes: nobody can attack it and its ships can't leave, so anything they build waits there. When they're ready, join their world to a neighbour in Hyperlanes above, and their ships set off by themselves. They start at war with everyone, pick their own name and colour the first time they log in, and can make alliances as usual.

Click an empty spot on the map for their world (the edge of the galaxy works best: it's easier to join to a neighbour later). It needs room round it, like other home worlds, and can't sit on a hyperlane.

Storage

The game and its battle replays are saved on Railway's disk (volume). Replays are kept up to 2 GB in all: past that (or if the disk gets nearly full) the oldest are deleted automatically, so the game can always save. Players who want to keep a battle can download its replay from the combat log.

Data sent to players

Railway charges for data the server sends out (egress), 5 cents a GB. This counts it since the server last started (every update restarts it).

WhatSentTimes

Game speed

How fast time runs for everyone: one galaxy, one clock. 1× is real time (a shipyard builds every 30 minutes). Speed it up to test, then put it back to 1× before your friends play for real. Players see the speed next to the galaxy clock.

Jump ahead

Skip game time forward at once. Everything due on the way happens: ships get built, fleets arrive, battles are fought (big battles take a moment each).
